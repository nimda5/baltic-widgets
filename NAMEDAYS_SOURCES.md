# Name-day research sources

This is the shared, growing directory for LV, LT and EE name-history research. Read it before a new round. Use [NAMEDAYS_BACKLOG.md](NAMEDAYS_BACKLOG.md) for the calendar queue, [NAMEDAYS.md](NAMEDAYS.md) for editorial/data rules, and the [round notes](research/namedays/) for individual claims and outcomes. Source URLs belong here and in working files/provenance, never in the widget interface.

Last directory review: **12 November 2026**, including the 12–13 November recheck and the 14–18 November research passes. Resource IDs below are stable references for research notes. A useful catalogue or repository is a place to search, not evidence for every name it contains.

## Start here

1. Search the exact NFC spelling, keeping diacritics. Try a second query without them only to discover candidates. Search the source language too: `personvārds`, `vārda izcelsme`, `vardas`, `kilmė`, `eesnimi`, `päritolu`.
2. Look for an exact dictionary entry or an explicit spelling correspondence. Read academic onomastics when the older dictionary lacks a form. Its absence does not prove that the name was unused.
3. Find a separate dated historical, archival, literary or professional episode. Identify whether it concerns this spelling, a documented related form, or a fictional character.
4. Inspect the original PDF page for diacritics, table columns, captions and author/date. Search-engine snippets and PDF text layers can drop or misread letters. Record both PDF and printed page numbers.
5. Save the exact URL, title/author/year, checked page or section, supported claim, limitations, outcome and next step in the next numbered round notes. Keep raw PDFs, HTML, OCR and screenshots in private `var/namedays-research/`.
6. A working comparison stays `needs_more` / `unverified`, even when a source independently establishes the spelling's use. Separate established facts from the unresolved inference. Do not transfer first attestations, calendar-entry years, biographies or population counts between forms.

The prose has Sanderss while the related list has Sanders; do not silently regularize the reference. **INT-BTN** curated [Dolores](https://www.behindthename.com/name/dolores), [Dolly](https://www.behindthename.com/name/dolly) and [Alexander](https://www.behindthename.com/name/alexander) support family context, without transferring dates or establishing a unique route into Latvia.

**LT-GUIDE** exact [Ginvydas](https://www.tevu-darzelis.lt/vaiku-vardai/ginvydas/) and [Ginvydė](https://www.tevu-darzelis.lt/vaiku-vardai/ginvyde/) explain **gin- / ginti, gina** and **vyd- / (iš)vydo**. Their historical paragraph uses LT-ELTA-CALENDAR’s dated press record. No exact Ginvydas/Ginvydė entry was found in the inspected Salys text. Commercial generated-looking claims about famous bearers were not accepted without primary confirmation. [Otas](https://www.tevu-darzelis.lt/vaiku-vardai/otas/) and [Otonas](https://www.tevu-darzelis.lt/vaiku-vardai/otonas/) supply the Germanic family connection, corroborated by curated **INT-BTN** [Otto](https://www.behindthename.com/name/otto); unsupported popular Greek-ear explanations were rejected.

**LT-SALYS83** pages visually checked: [PDF p. 310 / printed p. 295](https://tautosmenta.lt/wp-content/uploads/lituanistika/Salys_1983_LT.pdf#page=310) has the heading **LIEPA**, day **2**, and **Otonas (Otas)** in the 1964 calendar reprinted in 1983. This is **2 July**, not 30 June from nearby extracted context, and differs from the project’s 18 November. Preserve project membership/date; use this page for the explicit form relationship. [PDF p. 263 / printed p. 248](https://tautosmenta.lt/wp-content/uploads/lituanistika/Salys_1983_LT.pdf#page=263) gives **Romanas → Roman(us)** and **Romas** as a short form of both **Romanas and Romualdas**. Do not rename Romas-only bearers Romanas. **LT-VLE** [Otonas I](https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/otonas-i/) explicitly pairs him with **Otto I**, Holy Roman emperor **962–973**: a foreign historical example in Lithuanian, not a Lithuanian ruler or domestic first attestation.

**EE-MAGISTE29**, [PDF p. 15 / printed p. 13](https://www.etera.ee/api/oai2/download/10343.pdf#page=15), visually confirms **Ilo** in the feminine list, German **freude, freudebringerin**, joy / bringer of joy. The following line is **Ilts-u**, not Ilu. **INT-WIKTIONARY**’s exact [Estonian Ilo section](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Ilo#Estonian) instead derives it from **ilu**, beauty/splendour. Keep the distinct explanatory records rather than inventing a merged meaning or assuming the Finnish route. **POP-EE**’s opened [exact ILO statistics](https://www.stat.ee/nimed/ILO) records **23 men and 15 women on 1 January 2026**, and 18 November membership; the narrative uses only the both-sex fact. The dictionary’s masculine label does not negate historical and official female use.

The separate **LV-SILINS** [Latvian Ilo entry](https://www.izpetnieks.lv/en/name/Ilo), p. 161, supplies the inherited raw attestation “13th century, Ikšķile / third quarter of the 20th century, Cēsis” and cautions against combining records without a gender distinction. It discusses tentative Elias/Ilū comparisons. Neither its dates nor these tentative Latvian routes establish the Estonian name’s origin or first use. The formatter now keeps the first record’s century independent of the later record’s quarter; the raw source text and Latvian scope remain intact.

Unresolved exact forms: **Brīve/Brīvīte** catalogue p. 87 leaves origin unresolved and refers to Brīva; **Aleksandris/Alesandrs** pages point to an Aleksandrs explanation on p. 49 rather than establishing the exact forms; **Zandars** points to Zanders/Sanderss material on pp. 334/283. Exact **LV-LVA** searches using `search?locale=lv&words=on&sources=on&q=NAME` returned no entries for all five; absence is not proof of nonexistence. **Ilu** has no checked exact etymology: its Wiktionary URL returned 404, EKI direct fetch returned 403, and neither common-word ilu nor the Ilo entry proves a variant route. All six retain labelled hypotheses and individual next steps in `research/namedays/round18-notes.json` and backlog. Previously open names remain pending.

## Sources that did not resolve the current gaps

The 12 November rare LV names produced calendar-list matches, surname matches and unrelated common words. In particular, Vidavs as a surname does not establish the given name's origin. The VVC list confirms membership but leaves the five origins open.

For the remaining 13 November spellings, neither a shared day nor a nearby name in a catalogue proves a spelling correspondence. Automatic aggregators may merge Jevgenijs and Jevgeņijs, then display the latter's count under the former. Use such pages only to locate their underlying reference. Do not import merged counts, generic personality text, AI etymologies, greeting-shop descriptions or a search-result snippet as verified history.

Juozapotas now has an exact historical calendar record, but the proposed Juozapatas etymological bridge remains unverified. Commercial pages offer inconsistent derivations; do not collapse Joseph and Josaphat families on that basis. Its next step is an authoritative dictionary or explicit historical paired-form record, not another calendar listing.
